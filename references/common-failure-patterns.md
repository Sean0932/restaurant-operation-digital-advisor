# Common Failure Patterns

Use these patterns to diagnose symptoms. Adapt examples to the user's market.

## Inventory Is Not Trusted

Likely causes:
- receiving, waste, transfer, or count is incomplete,
- POS sales are not connected to recipe or stock deduction,
- correction rights are unclear,
- staff fall back to chat or memory.

First check:
- Pick 10 high-frequency SKUs and compare system stock, physical count, and the last 7 days of movement.

## Finance Reconciles Late or Manually

Likely causes:
- sales, payment, refund, inventory, procurement, and accounting use different time or category definitions,
- exception records are missing,
- accounting receives summaries without operational evidence.

First check:
- Trace one mismatch from the report back to the original order, payment, refund, inventory movement, or invoice.

## Stores Do Not Use the System

Likely causes:
- the workflow asks stores to enter too much,
- exception handling is harder in the system than in chat,
- store managers do not see value,
- responsibility and review are unclear.

First check:
- Observe one store for 3-7 days and record every time staff bypass the system.

## Vendor Demo Looked Good, Rollout Failed

Likely causes:
- demo covered standard flows but not exceptions,
- permissions and correction paths were not tested,
- implementation changed workflows without owner buy-in,
- integration details were assumed.

First check:
- Convert the last 3 painful exceptions into demo scenarios and ask whether the system can handle them end to end.

## Loyalty Campaigns Do Not Work

Likely causes:
- campaigns are not connected to store execution,
- coupon issuance, redemption, and repeat purchase are not tied together,
- store staff cannot explain or execute the rules,
- customer data is split across channels.

First check:
- Trace one campaign from send, receipt, redemption, order, store execution, and repeat visit.
